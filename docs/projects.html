Broward Airbnb market study

Four quarterly Inside Airbnb snapshots of Broward County, reduced to a straight answer for an individual investor: what an entire-home rental actually earns, which neighbourhoods clear their cost basis, and how much of the market multi-listing hosts already hold.

Four notebooks carry it end to end: the market itself, a data-quality decision memo, a segmentation by mixture models, and the investor guide.

Leaderboards

Every sport gets its own leaderboards: basic stats, advanced stats, betting, accuracy and results. Each row is scored against a stated baseline (the league mean, a coin flip, break-even, or the market) and its z-score sets the heat colour, so a number always reads against something.

Small samples are held back: a row under 30 games is never ranked or coloured. Early in a season the board shows early leaders instead, labelled as early. Every board has a table twin with every row and its notes.

The leaderboards run live on my own machine; the link opens a dated snapshot of them, built from the same code.

Pre-registered experiments

Each test writes down its question, data, seasons and pass mark before it runs, and scores its holdout once. A test passes only if it beats its baseline and the market by enough to matter.

Each one ships as a report: the question and verdict with one hero number, what was tested, the results, the checks against the baseline, the market and the holdout, what it means, the limits, and a table twin for every chart. Recent questions: whether pass-rush pressure adds points beyond the box score, and whether a starter's share of production moves with him after a trade or injury.
